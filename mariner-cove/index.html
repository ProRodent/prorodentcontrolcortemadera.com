Why waterside living changes the approach

  • Proximity to the water

    Shoreline vegetation and drainage channels give rats a route close to homes.

  • Low-lying landscaping

    Dense, water-adjacent plantings offer cover that drier inland yards do not have.

  • Docks and storage structures

    Boat storage and dock-adjacent sheds are common hiding spots near the water.

Mariner Cove sits close to the water, and that proximity shapes the kind of rodent activity homes in this part of Corte Madera tend to see. Shoreline vegetation, drainage channels and low-lying, damp landscaping all give rats cover that drier, more inland parts of town do not have to the same degree, and Norway rats in particular favor this kind of ground-level, damp environment.

Homes with a dock, boat storage, or a shed close to the water's edge deal with a specific version of the outdoor rodent problem. These structures often sit low to the ground with gaps that are easy for a rat to exploit and hard for a homeowner to check regularly.

What we typically see near Mariner Cove.

  • Norway rat burrows along drainage channels and low-lying fence lines.
  • Activity in dock storage boxes, boat covers and waterside sheds.
  • Mice moving into homes through gaps that also serve as drainage or utility routes.
  • Nesting in dense, water-adjacent landscaping close to foundations.

Treating a waterside property.

Drainage channels and low-lying areas get checked as part of any Mariner Cove inspection, since these features hold moisture and cover that draw rats even when the rest of a yard looks unremarkable. Boat storage and dock structures are treated as their own zone, separate from the main house, since access and placement both differ from a typical garage or shed.

Because ground-level dampness plays such a large role here, we also look at whether any standing water or poor drainage is making the situation worse, and flag that even though drainage repair itself falls outside our scope. Addressing both the rodent activity and the underlying moisture tends to produce a longer-lasting result than treating the rodents alone.

Sealing near the water.

Materials used for sealing near the water are chosen to hold up against damp conditions as well as gnawing, since a material that degrades quickly in a moist environment will not keep a rat out for long regardless of how well it seals initially. See our rodent exclusion service for the general approach, applied here with waterside conditions specifically in mind.

Seasonal changes near the water.

Rat activity near Mariner Cove often shifts with the tide and the season. Higher winter tides and heavier rains can push rats out of lower burrows toward higher ground, sometimes closer to a house than they would sit during a dry summer. A property that has never had indoor activity can see its first attic or garage signs during a particularly wet winter, which is worth knowing so a seasonal uptick is not mistaken for a new, unrelated problem.

We take tide and drainage patterns into account when placing outdoor stations here, since a spot that works well in summer can be underwater during a winter high tide, which wastes the placement and delays results.

If your property includes a private dock or shared access to one, checking it during the same visit as the house saves a separate trip and gives a complete picture of where activity is coming from along the waterfront edge of the property.

We leave a short written summary after each Mariner Cove visit noting what was found near the water's edge and any recommended follow-up, whether that means a seasonal recheck or a sealing job scheduled around the tide. Keeping that record on hand is useful for a waterside property dealing with recurring seasonal shifts.

A quick note on timing: low-tide hours make dock and shoreline inspection easier near Mariner Cove, since more of the ground-level structure is accessible than during a high tide. We schedule around that when the visit specifically involves a dock or waterside shed.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
