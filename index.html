How rodent control works in Corte Madera

Most calls we take here fall into a few patterns. Scratching in an attic at night. Droppings in a garage or pantry. A dead-animal smell that will not fade. Chewed wires in a car parked near Town Center at Corte Madera for a day or two. Burrow holes along a fence line close to the hillside below Ring Mountain Nature Preserve. Each one points to a different fix.

Mice and rats need different handling. Mice are small, breed all year indoors and slip through gaps near 1/4 inch, so they favor attics, garages and pantries. Rats are larger, climb well and follow food and water along fence lines, mature trees and open hillside. Our mouse control and rat control visits start from that split.

Corte Madera's mild, damp winters keep rodents active outdoors most of the year, and the mix of older homes on Christmas Tree Hill and Chapman Park, newer condos near The Village Shopping Center, and the open grassland at the base of Ring Mountain gives them plenty of cover. Homes closer to the hillside see more outdoor burrowing and yard activity, while older housing stock near the Old Town Square area sees more attic and crawl space calls as mice look for a stable indoor space.

Our four-step approach

  1. Inspect

    A full walk of the exterior and interior comes first: rooflines, vent screens, crawl space openings, foundation edges. Chapman Park's older housing stock gets extra attention at the sills and chimney flashing, since that is where decades of settling tends to open a gap.

  2. Treat

    Active animals get cleared using traps and tamper-resistant stations, positioned so kids and pets cannot reach them. Loose bait does not get used, and every placement gets pointed out and explained before the visit wraps up.

  3. Seal

    Hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar go over foam every time an opening gets closed, because foam alone gives way under a determined rat. On Corte Madera properties, that usually means work at the roofline or around an older, detached garage.

  4. Follow up

    A return visit checks the traps and stations and confirms the scratching, droppings or gnawing have actually stopped. When they have not, the priority is finding whatever gap got missed rather than reaching for another trap.

Signs you have mice or rats

Rodents leave clues long before you see one. These six are the ones we hear about most.

  • Droppings

    Mouse droppings tend to be small and tapered at the ends. Rat droppings are noticeably larger and rounded. Leave them undisturbed rather than sweeping.

  • Gnaw marks

    Fresh gnawing on wood, wiring, plastic or irrigation tubing looks light-colored and rough to the touch, and chewed wiring specifically raises fire risk.

  • Night noises

    A pattern of scratching or light scurrying inside walls or a ceiling after dark points to nesting nearby, not just a passing visitor.

  • Nests

    Torn paper, fabric scraps and pulled insulation bunched into a corner, box or engine bay mark an active nest site.

  • Grease marks

    A dark, greasy streak along a baseboard or pipe is a rub mark left by rodents reusing the same travel route night after night.

  • Odors

    A heavy ammonia-like smell points to an active population, while a sharper rotting odor in a wall or crawl space usually means something has died there.

Rodent control questions from Corte Madera homeowners

Do you respond at night or on weekends?

Yes, the line is staffed around the clock, and we offer same-day visits when the schedule has room. If you hear or see activity in a living space after dark, call right away rather than waiting until morning.

How do I tell mice from rats?

Look at the droppings and the size of any gap involved. A mouse leaves small, pointed droppings and can squeeze through an opening about the width of a pencil. A rat leaves larger, rounder droppings and tends to travel along fence lines and outdoor cover more than a mouse does.

Why do traps alone fail?

Traps clear out whatever is active right now but leave the actual opening untouched, so the next rodent that picks up the same scent trail gets in the same way. Closing the entry point is what actually stops the cycle.

Is treatment safe for pets and children?

We rely on tamper-resistant bait stations placed out of reach of kids, pets and wildlife. Tell us where pets and children spend time in the yard and we plan placements around those areas specifically.

What should I have ready when I call?

Your street address, where you have noticed activity, roughly when it started, and whether you have seen droppings, chewing or a dead animal. Let us know about pets, kids, and whether the home has attic or crawl space access.

Do you handle commercial properties?

Yes. Retail and restaurant spaces near Town Center at Corte Madera and The Village Shopping Center deal with the same pressures a home does. Our commercial rodent control service covers monitoring, treatment and the documentation many businesses need on file.

Do hillside homes near Ring Mountain need different treatment?

Often, yes. Open grassland and exposed rock give rats more natural cover near the preserve than a flatter, more built-up lot would see, so the inspection is planned around the yard edge and hillside side of the property specifically.
