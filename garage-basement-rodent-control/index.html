Why garages draw rodent activity.

A garage is often the least sealed part of a house. The main door rarely fits as tightly as an exterior wall, storage boxes give cover, and cars parked overnight offer a warm engine bay in cooler months. Mice tend to move along garage wall lines and behind stored items, while rats are more likely to enter through a gap under the main door or around a side entry, especially on properties near the hillside below Ring Mountain Nature Preserve.

Older attached garages near the Old Town Square area often have gaps around the shared wall with the house itself, which is one of the more common routes rodents use to move from a garage into living space.

What we check in a garage or basement visit.

  • Gaps under and around the main garage door.
  • Wall penetrations for utility lines shared with the house.
  • Stored boxes and materials that give cover for nesting.
  • Vehicle engine bays if a car is parked inside regularly.
A garage with one gap is rarely a garage with only one gap.

Protecting what's stored there.

Once activity is confirmed, traps and stations go along the actual travel route rather than scattered near stored items generally. If wiring, cardboard boxes or fabric items have already been chewed, we note that so you know what needs replacing versus what is still intact. Sealing the shared wall with the house and the main door gap is usually the step that keeps a garage problem from becoming a household one.

Basements and lower-level storage.

Where a home has a basement or below-grade storage area, the same principles apply: check the perimeter for gaps, look at stored materials for gnaw marks and nesting, and trace any activity back to its entry point before treating. Dampness in a lower-level space can also draw rodents looking for both water and shelter, so addressing any standing moisture is worth doing alongside pest control.

Garages attached to homes near open space.

Garages on properties that back onto the hillside below Ring Mountain Nature Preserve see a different pattern than garages closer to The Village Shopping Center. Hillside-adjacent garages more often show rat activity entering through a foundation gap or a gap under a side door facing the open space. Garages in denser, more built-up parts of Corte Madera more often see mice entering through the main door seal or a shared wall with the house.

Either pattern responds to the same basic approach: confirm the actual entry point rather than guessing, treat the active route, and seal it with materials sized to the animal involved. A garage that has had repeated activity over more than one season usually has more than a single entry point, so a full perimeter check rather than a spot fix is worth doing once activity is confirmed. Our rodent exclusion service covers that full perimeter work when a garage needs more than a single-point repair.

Seasonal storage and holiday decorations.

Garages that store holiday decorations, camping gear, or boxes that only get opened once a year are a common place for a mouse nest to go unnoticed for months. If you pull out a bin and find chewed cardboard or shredded packing material, treat that as a sign to check the rest of the garage rather than just cleaning up the one box, since the nest is usually still nearby.

Shared garages in condo and townhome buildings.

Condo buildings near The Village Shopping Center with shared underground parking or attached garage rows sometimes see activity travel between units through shared structural gaps. If your building has a shared garage and more than one unit is reporting activity, that is worth flagging to an HOA or property manager alongside your own service call. A building-wide check often resolves the issue faster than treating one unit at a time.

A garage visit typically wraps up with a short walk-through of what was found and what to expect next, whether that means a scheduled follow-up to check traps or confirmation that the space is clear. We leave notes on any repair recommendations even if the repair itself is booked as a separate visit, so nothing gets lost between the inspection and the fix.

Garage refrigerators and freezers, common storage items in Corte Madera garages, generate a small amount of warmth that can make the space behind or under the appliance an appealing nesting spot. Checking behind large stored appliances is a standard part of any garage inspection, even when there is no obvious sign of activity nearby.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Why does my garage keep getting mice even after I clean it out?

If the entry point under the door or through a shared wall is still open, clearing stored items removes hiding spots but does not stop new mice from getting in.

Can rats get into a basement through a window well?

Yes, a window well with a gap or a damaged screen is a realistic entry point, especially for a determined rat.

Should I worry about wiring in the garage?

Yes. Chewed wiring is both a fire risk and expensive to repair. Our rodent damage repair service handles fixing it after removal.

Do you treat detached garages the same as attached ones?

The approach is similar but the entry points differ, since a detached garage does not share a wall with the house but does have its own foundation and roofline gaps to check.

Is it safe to keep pet food in the garage?

Pet food is one of the more common things that draws rodents into a garage in the first place. Sealed containers are worth using regardless of current activity.
