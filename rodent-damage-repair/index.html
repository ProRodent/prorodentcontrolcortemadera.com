What rodents typically damage.

Mice and rats gnaw constantly to keep their teeth from overgrowing, and that habit does real damage over time. Insulation gets flattened and soiled by nesting, ductwork gets chewed through and loses efficiency, soffits and roofline trim get gnawed into new entry points, and drywall can get damaged from both chewing and water intrusion through a compromised roofline gap.

On older homes near Christmas Tree Hill and Chapman Park, we more often see soffit and roofline damage tied to years of small entry points going unnoticed. Newer construction closer to The Village Shopping Center more often shows isolated damage from a single recent entry point rather than years of accumulated wear.

Common repairs we handle.

  • Replacing chewed or soiled attic insulation.
  • Patching gnawed soffits and roofline trim.
  • Repairing small drywall damage from chewing or access points.
  • Assessing chewed ductwork for repair or replacement.
Repairing the damage without sealing the entry point just invites a repeat.

Why repair always follows sealing, not the other way around.

Repairing damage before the entry point is sealed risks the same spot being chewed open again. Every repair job on our end is paired with confirming that rodent exclusion has already addressed the entry point in question, so the repair actually holds rather than becoming a recurring cost.

Electrical and structural damage.

Chewed wiring is one of the more serious forms of damage, since it carries a fire risk in addition to the cost of repair. We document any wiring damage we find and coordinate with an electrician for the actual repair, since that work falls outside general pest and structural repair. Structural damage beyond drywall and insulation, such as significant framing issues, is handled similarly with a referral to the right contractor.

Timeline and what affects the cost.

Small repairs, such as patching a single gnawed soffit corner or replacing a section of insulation, are often done in the same visit as the exclusion work that seals the entry point. Larger jobs, such as widespread insulation replacement across an older attic near Chapman Park, or repairs that require coordinating with an electrician for chewed wiring, take longer and are scoped as a separate project once the extent of the damage is clear.

We always inspect before quoting a repair, the same way we do for rodent inspection and estimates, since damage that looks minor from below an attic hatch can turn out to be more extensive once insulation is pulled back. That inspection step also confirms whether rodent exclusion has already closed the relevant entry point, since repairing damage before sealing it risks the same spot being reopened.

A pending sale, refinance, or insurance claim often puts a deadline on repair work that would otherwise be scheduled more casually. Matching materials to the original construction.

Repairs on an older Corte Madera home near Christmas Tree Hill are done with an eye toward matching the existing materials where it matters for appearance, such as visible soffit trim. In places that will not be seen, like insulation inside a sealed attic, durability takes priority over an exact match. We discuss which areas call for a closer match before starting any visible repair work.

Photos taken before and after a repair are kept on file, which is useful both for your own records and for any inspection or insurance conversation that comes up later. Ask for copies at the time of the visit if you know you will need them for a specific purpose.

Repairs to shared structures, such as a fence line damaged by burrowing that is shared with a neighboring property, sometimes require a brief conversation between both homeowners before work begins. We are glad to explain the scope to a neighbor directly if that makes the repair easier to coordinate.

A final walkthrough after any repair confirms the work matches what was quoted and that nothing was missed, giving you a chance to ask questions before the crew leaves rather than after the fact.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Do you repair wiring yourselves?

We document chewed wiring and coordinate with an electrician for the actual repair, since that work requires a licensed electrical contractor.

Will insurance cover rodent damage repair?

Coverage varies by policy and by insurer, so check with your provider directly. We can provide documentation of the damage found, which is often needed for a claim.

Should entry points be sealed before or after repairs?

Before. Repairing damage without sealing the entry point risks the same spot being chewed open again shortly after.

How do I know if my insulation needs full replacement?

Insulation that has been flattened by nesting or heavily soiled no longer performs as it should, so replacement rather than partial cleaning is usually the better fix.

Can rodent damage affect a home sale?

Yes, an inspector may flag visible chewing, soiled insulation or damaged ductwork, so addressing it ahead of a sale is worth doing.
