Why commercial properties need a different approach.

A retail space or restaurant has different pressures than a house: regular deliveries, food storage, shared walls with other tenants, and higher foot traffic that can bring in activity from a neighboring unit. Documentation also matters more, since health inspections and lease terms often require a record of pest control activity, not just a one-time treatment.

Businesses at Town Center at Corte Madera and The Village Shopping Center, especially those near the Ellie the Elephant sculpture and other high-traffic areas, tend to see the most steady pressure from foot traffic and deliveries. Any Corte Madera business with a loading dock or storage room faces similar risk, regardless of how close it sits to the busiest retail corridors.

What a commercial visit covers.

  • Full walk-through of storage rooms, kitchens, loading docks and shared walls.
  • Monitoring station placement for ongoing tracking, not just a single treatment.
  • Written documentation suitable for a health inspection or lease requirement.
  • Coordination with property management when shared walls or common areas are involved.
A single treatment without ongoing monitoring rarely satisfies a health inspector.

Treatment and monitoring.

Traps and tamper-resistant stations are placed according to local food safety guidance, away from food prep and storage areas, and checked on a documented schedule. For businesses that need ongoing compliance records, monitoring continues on a set interval rather than stopping after the initial activity clears.

Sealing shared and commercial spaces.

Loading docks, shared walls with neighboring units, and older commercial buildings near downtown often have more entry points than a typical home, simply due to the number of doors, docks and utility penetrations involved. Our rodent exclusion approach applies the same principles at commercial scale, closing gaps with materials that hold up under repeated deliveries and foot traffic.

Documentation for inspections.

Every visit is documented, which matters both for health inspections and for lease agreements that require proof of active pest management. If you need records formatted for a specific inspection type, mention that when you call so we can provide what's actually required rather than a generic report.

Getting a quote for a multi-unit or franchise account.

Property managers and franchise owners with more than one Corte Madera location often want a single point of contact rather than separate bookings for each address. We can set up a standing visit schedule across several units and keep documentation consistent from one property to the next, which makes an annual health inspection or lease renewal easier to prepare for.

Pricing for a commercial account depends on square footage, the number of monitoring stations needed, and how often visits are required by a lease or local code. A first visit typically includes a full walk-through similar to our rodent inspection and estimates service, scaled to commercial square footage, so the monitoring plan that follows is sized to the real risk at that address rather than a generic package.

Working with property management.

Many Corte Madera commercial spaces sit inside a larger property with shared ownership or a management company overseeing common areas. When activity is likely coming from a loading dock, a shared trash enclosure, or a wall shared with a neighboring tenant, we coordinate directly with property management so the fix addresses the source rather than just the unit that happened to call. That coordination is especially useful for tenants who do not control the exterior of the building themselves.

New tenants and pre-lease checks.

A new tenant moving into a Corte Madera storefront or office sometimes requests a pest check before signing a lease, both to document existing conditions and to negotiate any needed remediation with the landlord ahead of move-in. That kind of pre-lease inspection follows the same walk-through process as an ongoing account, just scheduled as a one-time visit tied to the lease timeline rather than a recurring plan.

We also work with businesses that need to show a pattern of proactive pest management for insurance purposes, not just a reaction to an active problem. A documented, scheduled monitoring history is often exactly what an insurer or franchise auditor is looking for, separate from whether any activity has actually been found on a given visit.

Seasonal businesses, such as a shop that sees a heavy holiday season near Town Center at Corte Madera, sometimes schedule an extra monitoring visit ahead of their busiest weeks, when increased deliveries and foot traffic raise the odds of new activity. That kind of timing adjustment is easy to build into a standing account.

A short written summary after each visit, listing what was checked and what was found, gives a manager something concrete to reference between an owner's own site visits, rather than relying on a verbal update alone.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about commercial rodent control

Do you provide documentation for health inspections?

Yes, every visit is documented, and we can format records to match what a specific inspection type requires if you let us know in advance.

How often should a restaurant have monitoring visits?

It depends on the property and any lease or health code requirements, but ongoing scheduled monitoring is standard rather than a single treatment.

Can you work around business hours?

Yes, visits can be scheduled around operating hours to minimize disruption, including before opening or after close.

What if the activity is coming from a neighboring unit?

We coordinate with property management when shared walls or common areas are involved, since sealing on one side alone rarely solves activity coming from a shared space.

Is treatment safe near food storage and prep areas?

Yes, placement follows food safety guidance, keeping traps and stations away from food prep and storage while still covering the routes rodents actually use.
