Norway Rats vs. Roof Rats in the Corte Madera Area

Two different rat species show up around Corte Madera, and they use very different routes to reach a house.

Two species, two very different habits.

Both Norway rats and roof rats are found around Corte Madera, but they behave differently enough that treating them the same way often misses the mark. Norway rats are heavier, poor climbers, and prefer ground-level burrows near foundations, woodpiles and dense ground cover. Roof rats are leaner, excellent climbers, and favor high, raised routes like tree limbs, power lines and rooflines.

Where each species tends to show up locally.

Properties near the hillside below Ring Mountain Nature Preserve, with open ground and natural cover, tend to see more Norway rat activity, burrowing along fence lines and retaining walls. Neighborhoods with mature tree canopy, such as parts of Christmas Tree Hill and Chapman Park, see more roof rat activity, since overhanging branches give them a direct route onto a roofline without crossing open ground.

  • Norway rat signs: burrow holes at ground level, gnawing near foundations, activity in crawl spaces.
  • Roof rat signs: scratching in the attic, droppings near roof vents, gnaw marks on overhanging branches.
  • Both: grease marks along regular travel routes, chewed wiring or irrigation lines.
  • Both can be present on the same property if conditions favor each species in different areas.
A ground-level fix does nothing for a roofline problem.

Why the distinction changes treatment.

Trap and station placement differs by species since the travel routes differ. A Norway rat problem gets treated along the ground: fence lines, foundation perimeter and burrow entrances. A roof rat problem gets treated higher up the structure: attic access points, roofline gaps and the base of trees that provide a bridge to the house. Our rat control service starts by identifying which species, or both, is actually present before deciding where to place anything.

Sealing differs too.

Ground-level sealing for Norway rats focuses on foundation vents, gaps under sheds, and utility penetrations near ground level. Roofline sealing for roof rats focuses on soffit vents, roof edges and gaps where a chewed opening has already formed. Our rodent exclusion service scopes the work to match whichever species, or combination, an inspection actually confirms.

How population size differs between the two.

Norway rat colonies tend to be somewhat larger and more concentrated around a single well-established burrow system, since ground-level nesting supports a bigger group in one spot. Roof rats are more likely to be found in smaller, more dispersed groups spread across several nearby structures or trees, since climbing and travel at height favor a more distributed pattern than a single central burrow. This difference affects how a technician estimates the scope of a job once a species is confirmed.

Why misidentifying the species wastes time.

Placing ground-level traps for a roof rat problem, or focusing exclusively on roofline sealing when Norway rats are actually burrowing along a fence line, both waste effort on the wrong part of the property while the real activity continues unaddressed. This is one of the more common reasons a DIY trapping effort does not resolve an ongoing problem: the traps may be perfectly good ones, just placed based on an incorrect assumption about which species is actually present.

A trained eye can usually make the species call from droppings and travel signs within the first few minutes of an inspection. That is part of why booking a proper inspection, rather than guessing and buying traps at a hardware store, tends to save both time and money for anyone dealing with a rat problem for the first time.

How our services split along these lines.

Rat control covers both species under one service, since the diagnostic and treatment steps are handled by the same visit regardless of which one, or both, is found. Rodent exclusion is scoped differently depending on the finding: ground-level foundation and vent work for Norway rats, roofline and soffit work for roof rats. Backyard and outdoor rodent control becomes relevant specifically when Norway rat burrows are the primary finding, since that service focuses on ground-level yard treatment.

A note on prevention for each type.

Preventing Norway rats generally means managing ground-level conditions: keeping woodpiles off the ground, securing compost, and maintaining fence lines so gaps do not develop over time. Preventing roof rats leans more toward tree management, keeping branches trimmed back from the roofline, and checking roof vents and soffits for wear on a regular schedule, since these are the specific points roof rats exploit that ground-focused prevention does not address.

What this means if you are choosing where to focus first.

If you can only address one area right away, prioritize wherever you have actually seen or heard signs of activity rather than guessing which species is statistically more likely on a property like yours. A ground-level burrow demands ground-level attention regardless of how much tree cover a property has, and an attic full of droppings demands attic-level attention regardless of how open a yard might be. Let the actual evidence guide the first step rather than a general assumption about which species tends to show up more often nearby.

Over time, a property owner becomes more familiar with which pattern their own home tends to see. That history becomes a useful shortcut for future calls, letting a technician focus first on whichever species has caused problems before rather than starting from a blank slate every single time.

Can a property have both species at once.

Yes, and it happens more often in Corte Madera than many homeowners expect, particularly on a property that borders both open hillside and has mature tree cover near the roofline. In that situation, an inspection needs to check both ground-level and roof-level signs separately rather than stopping once one species is confirmed, since finding evidence of one does not rule out the other being active at the same time.

Either way, a documented history on file makes each future call more efficient, since a returning technician already knows which parts of the property to check first rather than repeating a full survey from scratch every time.

Basic safety steps while you wait for a visit.

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; disturbing them dry can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before a technician has assessed the property.
  • Wash your hands after touching anything near a suspected rodent area, even if you wore gloves.

Common questions

Can both species be in the same house at once?

Yes, especially on a property with both ground-level cover and mature trees near the roofline, conditions that favor each species differently.

Which species is more common in Corte Madera?

Both are present. Hillside and open-space-adjacent properties tend to see more Norway rats, while tree-heavy neighborhoods see more roof rat activity.

Do I need different traps for each?

Placement matters more than trap type in most cases, since the two species use different travel routes that determine where a trap actually needs to sit.

How do I know which one is in my attic?

Roof rats are the more likely attic occupant given their climbing ability, but a technician confirms this by droppings and travel signs during an inspection.
