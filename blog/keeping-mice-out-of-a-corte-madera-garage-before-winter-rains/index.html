Keeping Mice Out of a Corte Madera Garage Before Winter Rains

A garage is one of the easiest places for a mouse to slip into once the rainy season starts. A little prep before the first storm helps.

Why garages see a rainy-season bump.

As Marin County's rainy season sets in, mice looking for a dry place to shelter often find a garage more appealing than an open yard, especially if the main door has any gap along its bottom edge. A garage also tends to have stored boxes and clutter that give cover once a mouse gets inside, letting a small problem go unnoticed for a while.

The main door gap is the first thing to check.

Garage door weatherstripping wears down gradually and is easy to overlook until you specifically check it. A worn seal along the bottom of a garage door is one of the single most common entry points we find in Corte Madera garage calls, and it is also one of the easiest to fix before the rainy season really gets going.

  • Check the door sweep along the bottom edge for gaps or worn rubber.
  • Look at the corners where the door meets the frame, a common overlooked gap.
  • Check any side or back doors into the garage for the same wear pattern.
  • Look at where utility lines enter the garage from outside for unsealed gaps.
A worn door sweep is an open invitation.

Storage habits that help.

Keeping stored items in sealed plastic bins rather than cardboard boxes removes both a food source, if anything edible is stored, and an easy nesting material. Raising storage off the floor on shelving also removes some of the ground-level cover mice prefer, though it does not replace sealing the actual entry point.

Why the garage matters more than people expect.

Many homeowners think of the garage as a lower priority than the main house, since it usually does not hold living space. But an unaddressed garage entry point often becomes the route mice use to reach the rest of the house eventually, particularly in an attached garage sharing a wall with living space. Treating the garage as seriously as any other part of the home during prevention season tends to prevent problems from reaching the interior at all.

A simple annual checklist.

  • Replace a worn door sweep before the first fall rains rather than after noticing activity.
  • Check all garage-facing utility penetrations, including those for water heaters or laundry hookups.
  • Move firewood and yard waste storage away from the garage's exterior walls.
  • Do a quick visual check inside stored boxes for early signs of nesting or droppings.

None of these steps require professional help on their own, and doing them consistently each year, ideally in September, meaningfully reduces the odds of an active problem by the time the heaviest winter rains arrive. If you do find signs despite this kind of prevention, that usually points to a gap larger or less obvious than a worn door sweep, worth having a technician locate directly.

Garages with attached living space.

An attached garage that shares a wall with a bedroom or living area deserves extra attention during any prevention check, since a gap in that shared wall gives mice a much shorter route to living space than a gap in a detached structure would. If your garage is attached, checking the shared wall specifically, not just the main door, is worth the few extra minutes it takes.

What to do if you rent rather than own.

Renters do not always have the ability to replace a garage door seal themselves, but flagging a worn seal to a landlord or property manager before the rainy season is a reasonable request, framed as routine maintenance rather than a complaint about an active problem. Landlords generally prefer a small maintenance request over a larger pest control bill later in the season, so raising it early tends to get a faster response than waiting until activity is already confirmed.

A checklist to keep for next year.

Keeping a simple yearly note, even just a phone reminder set for late summer, of when you last checked the garage door seal and storage setup makes this an easy habit to maintain rather than something you remember only after noticing a problem. A five-minute check once a year is a small commitment against the alternative of dealing with an active mouse problem partway through the rainy season.

If you are unsure whether your garage door seal needs replacing, a simple test on a sunny day helps: close the door and look for any daylight visible along the bottom edge from inside. Visible daylight means a gap large enough to be worth addressing before the season turns, regardless of how the seal looks from outside.

Weatherstripping versus a full door sweep.

Weatherstripping along the sides and top of a garage door addresses a different set of gaps than the bottom door sweep does, and both are worth checking separately rather than assuming one covers the other. Side weatherstripping that has pulled away from the frame, often from years of sun exposure, creates a vertical gap that is easy to miss during a quick glance at the bottom seal alone.

Storage boxes stacked directly against the garage wall also deserve a look. That gap between boxes and drywall is a favorite travel corridor once a mouse is inside, and pulling boxes a few inches off the wall removes an easy hiding spot at almost no cost.

Common questions

When is the best time to check my garage door seal?

Late summer or early fall, before the rainy season really gets going, gives you time to replace a worn seal before it becomes an active entry point.

Does storing things in plastic bins actually help?

Yes, it removes both a potential food source and easy nesting material compared to cardboard, though it does not replace sealing the entry point itself.

What if I already see droppings in the garage?

That means prevention alone will not resolve it. A garage and basement rodent control visit addresses the current activity directly.

Is a garage door seal something I can replace myself?

Often yes, replacement door sweeps are widely available, though if activity is already present, sealing alone will not remove animals that are already inside.
