Why cars attract rodents.

Cars left parked for a stretch near Town Center at Corte Madera or The Village Shopping Center during a slow week are a common way a car ends up with a nest before anyone notices. The same risk applies to any vehicle kept in a garage that already has rodent activity of its own.

Once a nest is established, the risk is not just the mess. Rodents chew wiring, hoses and insulation, and a chewed wire near the engine can cause anything from a warning light to a genuine electrical or fire hazard.

Signs a rodent has been in your car.

  • Shredded material visible near the air filter or under the hood.
  • A strong odor coming from the vents when the air system runs.
  • Warning lights or electrical issues with no obvious cause.
  • Visible droppings on the engine bay floor or near the wheel wells.
A quiet engine bay is an easy place to hide a nest.

What a removal visit involves.

We check the engine bay, the area around the cabin air intake, and accessible spaces near the wheel wells for nesting material, droppings and gnaw marks. Nesting material is removed, and any chewed wiring or hoses are documented so you know what needs a mechanic's attention versus what is undamaged. We do not perform electrical repairs ourselves, but a clear list of what was chewed makes that visit to a mechanic faster.

Preventing it from happening again.

If the car is parked in a garage, checking that garage for its own entry points matters just as much as the vehicle itself, since a car parked in a garage with an active mouse or rat problem will likely see activity again. Our garage and basement rodent control service addresses that side of the problem.

What it typically costs to check and clear a vehicle.

A straightforward nest removal from an engine bay, with no significant wiring damage found, is usually a quick, affordable visit. Cost rises if nesting material has reached the cabin air system or if multiple areas of the vehicle show chewing, since that means more thorough removal and a longer damage list for your mechanic to review. We quote based on what we find during the check, not a flat fee regardless of severity.

If the vehicle is parked in a garage with other signs of activity, such as droppings near stored items or a gnawed box, it is worth booking garage and basement rodent control for the same visit. That way both the car and the space it sits in get addressed together.

Chewed wiring found during a vehicle check.

If wiring damage is significant enough to affect a system beyond what a mechanic handles, such as chewing that has spread into a firewall grommet or dashboard wiring harness, we note that separately so you have a full picture before deciding on repairs. Our rodent damage repair service covers structural chewing around a vehicle's parking area, such as a gnawed garage wall, though the vehicle's own wiring repair still goes to a mechanic.

Fleet and multi-vehicle checks.

Businesses with a small fleet of vehicles parked overnight near Corte Madera, such as delivery vans or service trucks, sometimes deal with recurring nesting across more than one vehicle at a time, especially if the vehicles are parked near a hedge line or storage area. A single check-and-clear visit can often cover several vehicles parked at the same location in one appointment.

If a vehicle has recurring nest problems despite repeated cleanups, that usually points to something in the parking environment itself, such as an unsealed garage or nearby woodpile, rather than the vehicle being an unusually attractive target on its own. Addressing that surrounding cause is often the more lasting fix.

Classic and collector cars stored for long stretches without regular driving are particularly vulnerable, since nothing disturbs a nest once it starts. Owners of a stored vehicle sometimes schedule a periodic check even without a specific concern, simply because the cost of a check is far lower than the cost of wiring repair after months of undetected activity.

Before we arrive

Questions about vehicle and car rodent removal

Can rodents in a car cause a fire?

Chewed wiring near the engine is a real fire risk if left unaddressed, which is why documenting and repairing any chewed wiring matters even after the nest is removed.

Do you repair the chewed wiring yourselves?

We remove the nest and document the damage, but wiring repairs are typically handled by a mechanic. A clear damage list from the visit speeds that up.

How do I know if the smell is from a nest or something else?

A musty, nest-like smell from the vents combined with any visible shredded material under the hood usually points to rodent activity rather than a mechanical issue.

Should I check my car if I park it in a garage with known rodent activity?

Yes, a car parked in an active garage is at real risk, and checking both the garage and the vehicle together is the more effective approach.

Is a car parked outdoors safer from rodents?

Not necessarily. An outdoor-parked car can still attract rodents seeking a warm engine bay, especially overnight or during cooler months.
