What the inspection covers.

An inspection is a full walk of the property, inside and out. That means the attic, any crawl space, the garage, kitchen and pantry areas, and the exterior perimeter including the roofline, foundation and any fence lines. The goal is to find every point of entry and every sign of activity, not just the one spot that prompted the call, since a house with one obvious entry point often has a second, smaller gap nearby that gets missed.

For Corte Madera addresses, that often means checking where older construction on Christmas Tree Hill and around Chapman Park has settled over the decades, since sills, chimney flashing and old plumbing chases open small gaps that are easy to miss without a close look. Newer condos near The Village Shopping Center get checked differently, with more focus on garage doors, dryer vents and shared-wall penetrations.

What we look for.

  • Droppings, sized and shaped to tell mice from rats.
  • Gnaw marks on wood, wiring, plastic or irrigation lines.
  • Grease smears along baseboards, pipes and fence tops.
  • Gaps around vents, utility lines, foundation cracks and roofline trim.
  • Nesting material in attics, garages, engine bays and storage areas.
You cannot seal a gap you never found.

What you get afterward.

After the walk-through, we explain what we found in plain terms: which species is likely involved, where the entry points are, and what a full treatment plan would cost before any work begins. There is no pressure to book on the spot. If you want to handle sealing yourself and just need the map of entry points, that is a fair use of the inspection too.

When to book an inspection instead of a specific service.

If you already know you have mice in the kitchen, booking mouse control directly usually gets a technician out faster. An inspection makes more sense when you are hearing noise but cannot tell where it is coming from. It also fits well when you are buying or selling a Corte Madera home and want a rodent-specific check, or when a previous DIY effort has not worked and you want to know why.

After the estimate.

If you decide to move forward, the same technician who did the inspection typically handles the follow-up work, which keeps the plan consistent from the first visit to the last. If you decide to wait, keep the notes from the inspection, since they still describe real conditions at your address and will save time if you call again later.

How an inspection connects to the next step.

Most inspections end with a clear recommendation: which service fits the findings, roughly how long the work will take, and what it costs. If mice are confirmed in a kitchen, that points to mouse control. If the activity is outdoors along a fence line, that points to backyard and outdoor rodent control instead. The inspection is designed to make that next decision easier, not to add an extra cost on top of whatever treatment you ultimately need.

For older Corte Madera homes near Christmas Tree Hill and Chapman Park, an inspection often turns up more than one issue at once, such as an attic entry point alongside early garage activity. Getting the full list up front, priced together, tends to save money compared to booking each fix separately as new signs show up over several months.

Inspections for rental properties.

Landlords and property managers with Corte Madera rentals sometimes book an inspection between tenants, both to document the condition of the property and to catch any activity before a new tenant moves in. That documentation can also help settle disputes over whether a rodent problem existed before or after a specific move-in date.

What happens if nothing is found.

Occasionally an inspection turns up no clear evidence of current rodent activity, even though a homeowner heard a noise or found something suspicious. In that case we say so directly rather than manufacturing a problem to justify a treatment plan, and we note what was checked so you have a record if the concern comes up again later.

An inspection report is yours to keep regardless of whether you book further work with us. Some homeowners use it to get a second opinion, and we have no problem with that. Our interest is in giving you an accurate picture of the property, not in pressuring a decision on the spot.

Inspections booked ahead of a known event, such as moving into a newly purchased Corte Madera home before unpacking, let you address any existing rodent activity before belongings are unpacked into the space. That is generally easier than treating around boxes and furniture already in place.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

How long does an inspection take?

Most Corte Madera homes take under an hour, longer if the attic or crawl space access is difficult or the property is larger.

Do I need to be home for the inspection?

It helps, since you can point out where you have heard or seen activity, but arrangements can be made if you cannot be present.

What does an estimate cost?

Ask about current pricing when you call, since terms can change. The important point is that you see the plan and the price before any treatment work begins.

What if you find more than expected?

We explain any additional findings and their cost before doing extra work, the same as with the original estimate.

Can an inspection tell me if I have mice or rats?

Yes. Droppings, gnaw marks and travel patterns differ enough between the two that a trained eye can usually tell which species is involved, or confirm both are present.
