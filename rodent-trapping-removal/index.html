Why placement matters more than trap count.

A dozen traps scattered around a garage do less than three traps set exactly where mice or rats are moving. Rodents travel along walls, fence lines and established runs rather than crossing open floor space, so the first job is finding that route: a baseboard with droppings, a fence top with a worn path, an attic joist with a grease smear. Traps go there, not wherever there happens to be room.

In Corte Madera, that often means placing indoor traps along kitchen cabinet toe-kicks and garage wall lines for mice, and outdoor stations along fence lines and the hillside edge below Ring Mountain Nature Preserve for rats moving in from open space.

The types of traps and stations we use.

  • Snap traps for fast-acting removal along confirmed indoor runs.
  • Tamper-resistant bait stations placed out of reach of children and pets.
  • Multi-catch traps for ongoing monitoring in garages and crawl spaces.
  • Glue boards only where snap traps are not practical, checked frequently.
A trap in the wrong spot just collects dust.

Checking and clearing on a schedule.

Traps are checked on a set schedule rather than left indefinitely. That keeps catches from sitting too long and lets us adjust placement quickly if a spot is not producing results while droppings keep appearing somewhere else. We track what is caught and where, which tells us whether the problem is shrinking or whether a new entry point has opened.

Trapping alone versus a full plan.

Trapping removes the animals that are already inside or in the yard, but it does not stop new ones from arriving through the same gap. For a lasting fix we usually pair trapping with rodent exclusion to close entry points, and with rodent inspection and estimates up front if you are not sure where the activity is coming from.

What to expect after trapping starts.

Catches usually drop off within the first couple of scheduled checks if the entry points are also being addressed. If catches stay steady, that is a sign there is still an open gap somewhere, and we go back to the inspection step to find it rather than just adding more traps in the same spots.

How trapping fits into a full visit.

Trapping is rarely booked entirely on its own for a first-time call, since a technician typically checks for entry points at the same time traps are placed. That combined approach means the first visit often includes both an assessment similar to rodent inspection and estimates and the initial trap placement, so you are not paying for two separate visits to accomplish what one visit can cover.

Ongoing trapping as a standalone service makes more sense for commercial accounts or properties with a history of recurring activity, where scheduled monitoring visits are already part of the plan. For a typical Corte Madera home dealing with a first mouse or rat problem, trapping paired with exclusion in a single project is usually the faster and more cost-effective path.

When trapping alone is the right call.

Not every situation needs a full exclusion project right away. A tightly built newer home near The Village Shopping Center with a single confirmed entry point sometimes only needs trapping plus a simple seal, rather than a full perimeter review. We size the recommendation to what the inspection actually finds rather than defaulting to the largest possible scope of work.

Tracking results across multiple visits.

For properties on a recurring monitoring schedule, we keep notes from one visit to the next: what was caught, where, and whether activity is trending down. That record makes it easy to tell whether a treatment plan is working or whether it is time to revisit the inspection and look for a gap that has not been addressed yet.

If a scheduled check finds a trap that has gone untouched for several visits in a row, we usually relocate it rather than leaving it in a spot that clearly is not seeing activity. Adjusting placement based on real results, rather than sticking to the original plan out of habit, is part of what keeps a trapping program effective over time.

For a vacation home or a property that sits empty for stretches of the year, a trapping and monitoring visit timed just before the owner returns can confirm the property is clear before anyone moves back in. That is a common request among part-time Corte Madera residents.

A trapping schedule can also be adjusted around a homeowner's own travel plans, front-loading checks before a trip and picking back up on return, so a property is never left with an unmonitored trap sitting for an extended stretch while nobody is there to notice.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent trapping and removal

How many traps does a typical home need?

It depends on the size of the property and how many active routes we find, usually a handful placed precisely rather than a large number placed loosely.

Are the traps safe around pets?

Tamper-resistant stations and careful indoor placement are used specifically to keep pets and children away from traps. Tell us about pets so placement accounts for them.

How often are traps checked?

On a set schedule agreed to at booking, which keeps catches from sitting too long and lets us adjust placement if needed.

Can I set my own traps between visits?

You can, but tell us so we can account for it and avoid duplicating effort or missing where activity is actually concentrated.

What happens once trapping stops producing catches?

We confirm activity has stopped and typically recommend sealing any remaining entry points, if that has not already been done, so new animals cannot move in the same way.
